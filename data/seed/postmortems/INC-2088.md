## Summary

A producer change switched the partition key from `order_id` to `merchant_id`.
The top merchant accounts for 38% of orders, so one partition took a third of
all traffic while the other 23 sat idle. Consumer lag on that partition grew to
1.4M messages; order confirmation emails were delayed up to 90 minutes.

## Symptoms

- orders-consumer group lag climbing steadily from 09:05
- Lag concentrated on partition 7 of orders-events
- kafka-broker-4 disk write throughput at 3x its usual rate
- No consumer errors - throughput ceiling, not failures

## Root cause

Partition key changed to a high-cardinality-but-heavily-skewed field. A single
consumer instance can only process one partition's worth of traffic, so adding
consumers did not help.

## Remediation

1. [diagnose] Confirm skew rather than consumer failure `kafka-consumer-groups --describe --group orders-consumer` — @tomas
2. [mitigate] Scale orders-consumer from 6 to 12 instances — @tomas (outcome: no effect; the hot partition was the bottleneck)
3. [fix] Revert the producer partition key to `order_id` — @tomas (outcome: new traffic spread evenly across 24 partitions; lag started draining at 09:52)
4. [mitigate] Run a one-off backfill consumer pinned to partition 7 — @rhea (outcome: backlog cleared by 11:20)

## Prevention

- Partition-key changes require a skew estimate in the design review
- Per-partition lag alerting, not just per-group
