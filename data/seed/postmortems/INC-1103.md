## Summary

The settlement batch and the refund worker updated the same `orders` rows in
opposite order, producing deadlocks. Postgres killed one side of each pair;
the batch retried, amplifying the contention. Settlement finished 90 minutes
late. No customer-visible errors.

## Symptoms

- `deadlock detected` in orders-db logs, roughly 40 per minute
- settlement-batch job duration alert
- Elevated lock waits on db-prod-01

## Root cause

Two writers took row locks on `orders` in inconsistent order. Under the volume
of a month-end settlement run the interleaving became frequent enough to
deadlock continuously.

## Remediation

1. [mitigate] Pause the refund worker to break the contention — @lena (outcome: deadlock rate fell to zero within a minute)
2. [fix] Order all row updates in both jobs by `order_id` ascending — @lena
3. [fix] Add `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` with a consistent sort in the settlement batch — @lena

## Prevention

- Lock-ordering convention documented for anything writing to `orders`
